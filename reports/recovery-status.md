## The candidate recovery design (R1 accepted 2026-10-08)

R1 licenses one claim: a candidate recovery design exists for this body. The design is SD boot, entered by the documented press of the Function button and command dial (E-SD-BOOT-LEAD, `reported`: the manual names no dial, rotation, direction, duration or release; the press was exercised on this body 2026-10-09 in every reading the documentation supports and did not reproduce (E-SD-BOOT-ENTRY-HOLD-150, hardware-observed; the owner observation section below)), in either ROM-decoded variant: a FAT volume carrying a survivable `0x80000304` register configuration (`ffsys.bin`) and a bounded staged record (`ffboot.bin`), or the mode-2 raw layout with the config in blocks 0 and 1 and the record from block 2, with the block count from the config block. Modes 1 and 2 run before the application, read no internal media and jump into what they staged ([window map](bootrom-window.md) sections 1 and 3), which is the property that makes the routes application-independent.

The mode-4 decode supplies the stock internal-media boot contract: the marker and config fields of the scanned page, 128 KiB page groups, the trailer `0xaaaa` gate at media `0x24a0000` and the segment table at media `0x140000` ([window map](bootrom-window.md) section 9, [UPDATE-01](update-validation-and-boot.md), byte-read). Those gates are not a format requirement for a project-authored SD diagnostic: mode 1 stages the directory-declared `ffboot.bin` size and mode 2 stages the config-declared raw block count without validating a record header, checksum or trailer (window map sections 3.2-3.3, byte-read).

Bounds on the design and the claim:

- The documented strap values remain a lead (E-SD-BOOT-LEAD), not a captured selector on this body. Entry on this body needs rapid power toggling with Fn plus the inward sub-command-dial press held: cold starts and slow toggles have never entered, and toggling at about 0.75 second per transition, OFF included, has entered within a few switch-ons in every session (E-SDBOOT-ENTRY-TIMING-150). Stage-marker cards place the cold failure before config-apply offset `0x80000510`, in the ROM's card bring-up, FAT/`ffsys.bin` read or the first clock waits (E-SDBOOT-STAGE-MARKER-150, [section 2.61](sd-boot-card-entry.md#261-entry-timing-and-stage-markers)). FAT versus raw route and the physical cause remain unbound.
- The diagnostic card is buildable from held material (static-inference, E-SDBOOT-CARD-BUILDABLE-150, [per-file verdict and FAT contract](bootrom-window.md#32-per-file-buildability-verdict)): `ffsys.bin` is the 1024-byte concatenation of `payload.bin[0x48,0x248)` and `[0x848,0xa48)`, preserving the seven pair writes and every config-driven field of the hardware-observed mode-4 apply (E-CONFIG-APPLY-150); `ffboot.bin` can be project-authored bounded A32 code; `ffuser.bin` is not read by ROM modes 1, 2 or 4. The startup-restored blink and report-sector payloads are built and reviewed. Physical reporter writes have not been observed. The repeating marker establishes execution; mode-1/2 selection and configuration effects remain unbound.
- The minimal marker design does not itself supply an internal-media writer. The later exact recorder-v2-v5 repair adds a separately evidence-gated physical-block writer; it is not a generic reflash path.
- R2 is demonstrated by the bounded marker. The later independent bootstrap loads installed native storage, restores the recorder target call in RAM and resumes to UI, demonstrating scoped R3 reachability (E-STAGED-RECOVERY-SUCCESS-150). Neither establishes a general recovery loader or a reproducible cold-entry recipe; formal R4 acceptance remains unclaimed.

A recovery-relevant load hazard bears on the mode-4 internal-media stage, not the FAT or raw SD stage: an unselectable page inside `[0x40000, 0x80000)` shifts every later page down 128 KiB and the load still returns success; the ROM applies no checksum, and the media-side trailer and segment gates do not catch a compacted stage ([window map](bootrom-window.md) section 9, E-BOOTROM-MODE4-150, byte-read). Mode 1 instead follows the FAT chain and has its own unchecked file size and sector-rounded DMA destination span (window map section 3.3); successful transfer is not executable-range validation on either route.

## What the leads establish

Pinned fffw `bedc091e0b54a1a34aaf6929dd08e1db36d13b08` README describes `ffre` as unpublished/abandoned and only partially tested on X-E2. It lists X-M1 as potentially adaptable, not demonstrated. `boot/README` asks ffun to create `ffsys.bin`; the pinned public parser/manifest/decompressor needed for that is missing. Thus there is no reproducible X-M1 boot/recovery build in that source.

The [maintainer's Route 0xff80 notes](https://github.com/tiredboffin/fffw/wiki/Route-0xff80) report an X-E2 ROM with multiple storage boot paths, independent SD LED/file experiments and a prospective native-updater approach left unfinished. The [EXR II CPU page](https://github.com/tiredboffin/fffw/wiki/EXR-II-CPU) reports Cortex-R4 feature registers and a boot-source selector. These sibling findings supply search priorities; they do not establish X-M1 CPU, register addresses, storage layout or entry conditions. No immutable wiki revision was acquired, so these remain dated reported leads, separate from the pinned code inventory.

The inspected X-E2 boot example disables interrupts, sets a stack and writes initialization/GPIO registers before and inside the C body. Its halt path also writes before WFI. These sibling source effects demonstrate why a copied diagnostic needs a complete initialization-write audit; they supply no X-M1 addresses, electrical mapping or approved execution. Nothing was compiled or run.

The pinned X-E2 `bootrom.h` enumerates candidates RAM, MMC0 FAT, MMC1 raw and EEPROM variants; an enum does not trace reset execution or establish this body uses any particular storage controller. X-M1 boot-ROM bytes now exist in this project: the reviewed `probe-bootrom` capture (E-BOOTROM-PROBE-150) holds the 0x4000 bytes at `0x80000000`, whose SHA-1 and `JASM_A_1` footer match the `ff80 bootrom` lead that lists X-M1 in its allowlist, so the lead is hardware-observed for this body rather than an allowlist string. The window is an ARM vector table plus boot code, register-base tables consumed by the hardware-config apply and the SD-BOOT filenames `ffsys.bin` and `ffboot.bin` in the open, which puts the manual's factory path in ROM bytes; [the window map](bootrom-window.md) decodes it and lists the outward references the next ROM capture must answer.

The owner-purchased X-M1 service manual ([S15](../spec/09-sources.md)) documents the strongest X-M1-specific lead so far, a factory SD-BOOT path called "MAIN PWB Initialize". An SD card (FAT32) holds the service files `ffboot.bin`, `ffsys.bin` and `ffuser.bin` (package ZJ02217, distributed only on Fujifilm's service server and not obtained). The camera is powered on while pressing the Function button and command dial, with no USB cable. The manual's verb is "pressing"; it names no rotation, direction, duration or release and does not say which of the two command dials or whether the press is a centre click or a press of the dial body, so the actuation beyond the press is unspecified. The LED runs green blinking, red, orange blinking, orange, orange/green blinking, then steady green, and the camera is then switched off. The manual states that a new main board carries no firmware, so this path installs firmware onto blank flash without the application firmware or USB. It also "resets the flash ROM and formats the internal memory" and returns all adjusted (calibration) data to defaults. Restoring calibration then needs the full Fujifilm adjustment sequence: mount data, CMOS defect correction, camera, flash and battery adjustment, with per-body MOUNT data files. The manual's separate firmware-download item ends with an "Adjustment Value Protection Reset", which resets the EEPROM while keeping adjustment values; that shows the firmware separates calibration from other EEPROM content. These are documentary claims, not demonstrated. The SD-BOOT file format, any signature or model check, and the boot-ROM trigger conditions are unknown. Using this path for recovery would require a prior body-specific calibration backup and a way to restore it. Attempting the button combination with an SD card holding unknown files is H3.

The same manual says that if End Setting is skipped after the adjustment software has exchanged data with the camera, the camera stays in repair (jig) mode and is not recognized as a PTP or mass-storage device. The owner's observation (`E-JIG-OBSERVED`) differs: after jig entry with no adjustment software, a plain power-off and normal power-on gave normal USB enumeration, at the cost of a user-configuration reset (date, language, custom slot). That suggests jig entry alone doesn't set the repair-mode flag; this is inference from one uninstrumented observation. Pinned fffw (`bedc091`, and experimental `469c3ad`) has no End Setting or mode-switch code: its `jig on/off/status`, `load`, `save`, `key` and `media` commands are declared in the argument parser but unimplemented. The nearest implemented operation, `cfgdata save` (app80 request `0x22`, configuration RAM to EEPROM), is a persistent write.

Fujihack core `754acff9aa0322df19446cf7eca699b221c3330d` has no X-M1 port/header. Its [supported list](https://wiki.fujihack.org/supported/) explicitly distinguishes RTOS-family listings from working ports. The separate patcher `0e81431c32f2fc908d9626d255e1d758ebfe8a79` byte-complement export supplies no ROM/boot prerequisites and validates no physical storage writer. Selected exact source hashes are in `third_party/firmware-source-inventory.json`; no upstream imports/programs/builds besides the audited header/export chain were executed.

### Factory SD-BOOT path (service manual, S15)

The service-manual route has now been attempted with project-authored substitute files, not the unavailable factory package. An exact independently reviewed FAT32 image on a 7.2 GB SDHC card produced no LED or LCD response under the documented-button interpretation; its complete post-run capture was byte-identical and all four project report slots remained absent (E-RECOVERY-FULL-RAM-FAT32-DARK-150). This is not evidence that the factory ZJ02217 package would fail.

- **Entry:** an SD card holding the SD-BOOT files `ffboot.bin`, `ffsys.bin` and `ffuser.bin` (service package ZJ02217, Fujifilm service web server; not obtained), then power on while pressing the Function button and command dial. No USB is connected during this step, and the manual names no dial, rotation, direction, duration or release, so which dial and which press shape the entry needs are unspecified. The manual's LED sequence is green blinking → red → orange blinking → orange → orange/green blinking → green.
- **Physical result:** the exact FAT32 full-RAM substitute was verified again after card removal/reinsertion, then tried once with Fn, inward sub-command-dial and full shutter. The body remained dark, and the captured card remained byte-identical. FAT16 formatting and use of a 64 GB SDXC card therefore do not explain this result. Whether the ROM sampled mode 1 remains unresolved.
- **Factory scope:** the manual specifies this step for a new MAIN PWB without firmware. It does not establish that project-authored substitutes reproduce the factory package.

## Owner observation: power-on presses with no card (2026-10-09, hardware-observed)

The owner ran the assessed no-card exercise on 2026-10-09 in a broader form than proposed (E-SD-BOOT-ENTRY-HOLD-150, hardware-observed): with no card and no USB at any step, he powered the body on while pressing the Function button together with each button on the camera except the flash release, and together with the sub-command dial press. Every combination booted the application; all but one landed in normal operation, and the exception, Function plus DISP/BACK, reached the ordinary firmware-upgrade screen, an application surface reached after a normal boot (the boot key test that selects it is application code, E-UPDATE-ENTRY-KEY-BIND-150), so that combination also ran the mode-4 path. No combination produced a dark or halted body.

Why normal boots are evidence about the strap bits: the mode selection is read at reset from `0xfff89014` bits 11:10 and `0xfff98000` bits 2:0 before any media is examined (byte-read, `0x80000274`/`0x80000278`, called at `0x80000130` before the dispatch), the dispatch runs one handler per boot, and with no card the application is reached only through the successful mode-4 handoff (byte-read: `0x80000130`-`0x8000021c` halts at `0x80000020` on any nonzero return with no fall-through; modes 1 and 2 exhaust their bounded no-card retries and return -1). The cardless runs of modes 0, 3, 5, 6 and 7 are not fully decoded (static-inference: none of them stages the mode-4 record), so the reading rests on identifying the observed normal boots with mode 4, not on an absence of dark halts. Every combination tried held the mode-select inputs at their mode-4 values.

What it excludes: the tested no-card/no-USB combinations did not select a ROM SD route. They included Fn plus sub-command-dial push. This is evidence-negative for those cardless trials, not for the card-present or battery-retained conditions that subsequently produced payload execution.

What it leaves open: the card-present Fn plus sub-command-dial push, undocumented actuations, reset-time pad driving and selector writeability/retention. The decoded main CPU selects the route before reading media; it does not inspect files to enable entry. That ordering does not rule out card-dependent hardware or companion-controller behavior before the sample. The cardless observation cannot close those conditions.

The cardless sweep is not repeated. Later card-present, battery-retained execution establishes an early foothold, but the successful switch edge and mode-1 versus mode-2 selection remain unresolved. The X70 record-button identity is not transferred to X-M1. [The selector findings](sd-boot-card-entry.md#2-what-drives-the-two-sampled-fields-at-reset) and [hardware-route map](recovery-hardware-routes.md) retain those distinctions. A dark halt cannot identify a selected mode; a RAM signature overwritten by ordinary boot cannot establish payload execution (E-SDBOOT-RAM-SIGNATURE-150, [detection trace](boot-card-detection.md)).

## The Function plus DISP/BACK result

Function plus DISP/BACK reached the ordinary firmware-upgrade screen (E-SD-BOOT-ENTRY-HOLD-150, hardware-observed), the screen [the install procedure](install-procedure.md) enters with DISP/BACK alone. The entry decision does not read the Function button: the boot poller's DISP/BACK branch requires key state `DISP_B` (`0x9`) behind configuration byte `0x0096b429` bit 1 and raises the boot mode to 8, no test of `FUNCTION` (`0x28`) or `FUNCTION2` (`0x219`) appears in the boot key fold or the poller, and the mode-8 arm of the UI dispatcher consults no key state (byte-read; E-UPDATE-ENTRY-KEY-BIND-150, whose branch-to-screen binding is static-inference). A Function press is folded into the key-state bitmap like any pressed key but is not consulted on this path, so the owner's combination is the documented DISP/BACK entry with Function ignored. This is an ordinary-firmware surface reached after the application boots, not the ROM SD-BOOT path the recovery design needs.

## Native-function feasibility handoff

The delivered stock case is the 1.50 area-3 record read-validation callback `0x006a84d4` (E-AREA3-VALIDATOR-NATIVE-150, accepted for G3 2026-10-08, [case](../emulation/README.md#stock-native-case-150-area-3-record-validation)); no further exact stock function is yet eligible. The 1.50 load map supplies addresses, and UPDATE-01 identifies checksum helper `0xdb89a4` as a candidate whose expected arithmetic can be specified independently. CPU/mode, ABI, transitive calls and globals still require a justified case; its scheduler yield cannot be silently stubbed as proof of stock behavior. Sibling Cortex-R4 reports cannot supply those facts. D-01 can proceed with explicitly synthetic CPU fixtures.

| Priority after mapping | Reason to consider | Required independent evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pure checksum/byte comparison/string primitive | Small inputs, deterministic outputs, few globals and no peripherals | Exact instructions/function boundary/calling convention plus externally specified arithmetic or known-library behavior; avoid generic success stubs |
| Container/range validation helper | Useful bounded recovery-parser behavior | Understood format/checksum contract, every transitive input/table/call and wrong-model/truncation cases |
| AF arithmetic/decision primitive | Closer to AF goal while avoiding drivers | Identified units/freshness/global-state contract and an independently known stock result |
| Boot/updater/event/lens paths | High dependencies and hardware effects | Defer until controllers, scheduler/interrupt/DMA, storage exclusions and completion/error semantics are understood |

Source inspection and candidate ranking are H0 planning, not native stock behavior or measured AF improvement. Input identities and reproduction belong in the corresponding technical report and the evidence ledger.
